# CourseCompass

Independent UX Research + Data Analytics + Product Development Project

Tagline: Discover your direction.

I think we should treat CourseCompass like a real startup-style product project, where you're both the researcher and developer. The goal isn't just to create a website. It's to identify a genuine student problem, use research and data to understand it, and build a personalized solution.

## 1. Define the project

## CourseCompass

The problem

College students have hundreds of courses, majors, and academic pathways to choose from, but existing planning tools tend to emphasize graduation requirements rather than personal interests, learning preferences, and career goals.

The solution

A personalized academic discovery platform that helps students find courses, explore majors, and discover academic opportunities based on who they are and what interests them.

Target users

Undergraduate students, especially first- and second-year students who are exploring majors, choosing electives, or figuring out their academic direction.

My recommendation: Start with UF as a pilot university, using a manageable selection of real courses. We can expand later.

We also need to make sure course information comes from an appropriate source and that prerequisites are represented accurately. We shouldn't claim to predict whether a student will enjoy a course; we'll explain how recommendations match their stated preferences.

## 6. Start here: Research planning

Before coding anything, we should answer four questions:

1. How do students currently discover electives and explore majors?
2. What frustrates them most about the process?
3. What information matters most when choosing a class (interest, workload, career relevance, professor, schedule, etc.)?
4. Would students trust personalized recommendations, and what would make those recommendations useful?

For research, I'd aim for 6–10 interviews and, if feasible, 30–50 survey responses. These are practical targets for an exploratory project, not statistically representative samples. We'd keep participation voluntary and avoid collecting unnecessary personal or academic information.
